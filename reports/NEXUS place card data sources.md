# Build a place card that tells the truth

A 315 px NEXUS place card is feasible today with keyless and bring-your-own-key (BYOK) sources, but only four of the five requested facts can be real. **Distance, address, bike and car time, and a photo have workable fallback chains. Live fares from Rapido, Uber and Ola do not.** No public, legitimate fare feed exists for any of the three in India. Uber's price endpoint needs business approval and its terms bar competitor price comparison. Ola's estimate API is reported invite-only. Rapido has no official API, and its only known fare endpoint is reverse-engineered and reportedly breaking ([Uber docs](https://developer.uber.com/docs/riders/references/api/v1.2/estimates-price-get), [apis.io](https://apis.io/providers/ola/), [Skills Directory](https://www.skillsdirectory.com/skills/ankitjh4-rapido)). The recommended design therefore shows a clearly labelled "estimate, not a live price" fare range built from distance, time and a published tariff, plus "open in app" buttons so the user sees each app's real price there. Google Routes and Places, with the user's own key, give the best quality (about 1,000 to 10,000 free calls per month depending on field tier). The keyless chain (OSRM, Nominatim, Wikipedia, satellite tiles) works for distance, address and landmarks, but not for shop photos or bike time. Everything here comes from research notes written in November 2026; the notes themselves flag many items as unverified, and this report keeps those flags.

## Distance, time and address: a four-rung chain from Google to haversine

Distance and travel time are the most solvable fields. With a Google key, a Routes API `computeRoutes` call for `DRIVE` and another for `TWO_WHEELER`, each with the field mask `routes.duration,routes.distanceMeters`, costs about two calls per card. Compute Routes has monthly free caps of 10,000 (Essentials), 5,000 (Pro) and 1,000 (Enterprise) ([Google pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). A field mask is mandatory ([Choose fields](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/choose_fields)). `TWO_WHEELER` is a genuine motorcycle mode, distinct from bicycle, with an India (Kolkata) example in the docs. It is beta, Google requires a visible warning to the user, and it is billed at a higher rate than other modes ([Google two-wheel routing](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/route_two_wheel)). Whether India is on the supported-region list, which SKU tier TWO_WHEELER falls in, and whether traffic-aware works for it were not verified (confidence: medium that it works in India, low on cost). Without a Google key the notes recommend OpenRouteService (free key, 2,000 directions per day, 40 per minute, non-commercial) for car, then the public OSRM demo server (car only, 1 request per second, a real User-Agent, ODbL attribution, "reasonable non-commercial use", no uptime guarantee) ([ORS plans](https://openrouteservice.org/plans/), [OSRM demo server](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Demo-server), [OSRM usage policy](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Api-usage-policy)). The last rung is haversine straight-line distance multiplied by about 1.3, labelled "straight-line approx". The 1.3 factor is a rule of thumb with no source; no road-to-straight-line ratio for Indian cities was found.

Bike time is the weakest keyless field. No verified free hosted service offers a motorbike profile. Valhalla has `motor_scooter` and a beta `motorcycle` costing, but only self-hosted use was documented, and a public instance was not verified ([Valhalla API](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/route/api-reference/)). GraphHopper's free tier lacks motorcycles, and ORS lists only car, cycling and walking profiles. The honest keyless answer is a car-derived time shown as "about" with an assumed urban-speed band, and never a cycling-profile time presented as motorbike time. No source validates a car-to-motorbike time factor, so any factor is an unvalidated assumption. OSRM durations are free-flow with no live traffic (inferred from its design, not stated on the pages fetched), so every non-Google duration needs a "no live traffic" label.

For the address, the recommended order is Google `formattedAddress` (Essentials tier, 10,000 free per month) when keyed, then Nominatim reverse geocoding, then Photon. Nominatim's public policy allows at most 1 request per second, requires an identifying User-Agent, requires caching, forbids autocomplete and grid-style reverse geocoding, and requires ODbL attribution ([OSMF policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/)). Photon's public instance is fair-use with no availability guarantee, and its official terms were not read ([Photon README](https://github.com/Komoot/photon)). Opening hours, phone and rating are Enterprise-tier Google fields (1,000 free per month), so fetch them only when the card opens and the user has a key ([Place Details](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/place-details)). Keyless, Nominatim `extratags` or an Overpass lookup may return `opening_hours` and phone where OSM has them. Coverage for Indian shops is unmeasured and likely sparse, so omit the row when absent. The user's current location is needed for every distance, and the notes do not cover how NEXUS obtains it. The existing Maps work reads "no device location" in some features, so the origin source (spoken, saved home, or OS location) is an open design question.

## Photos: Google when keyed, Wikipedia for landmarks, an honest map for shops

A single representative photo is the hardest field to source legitimately. Google Places (New) has the best coverage: Text Search with the field mask `places.id,places.displayName,places.photos`, then a GET on the `/media` URL with `maxWidthPx` (1 to 4800). Pricing: Place Details Photos is Enterprise tier with 1,000 free events per month, then $7 per 1,000 ([Place Photos](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/place-photos), [pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). Two rules matter: photo names "cannot be cached" and expire, so fetch on demand and keep only place IDs, and non-empty `authorAttributions` must be displayed with the image. Whether the Text Search `photos` field bumps that call to Enterprise, and whether a key restricted to one API works for `/media`, were not confirmed. The full Google terms text could not be retrieved either, so the caching rules rest on the Place Photos page alone.

Keyless, the Wikipedia REST summary endpoint returns a thumbnail, original image and Wikidata ID. The notes verified it for Charminar: a 330 px thumbnail, a 2174 by 3071 original and Wikidata item Q1771696 ([REST summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/Charminar)). Wikimedia requires a descriptive User-Agent with contact details ([policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Wikimedia_Foundation_User-Agent_Policy)), and RESTBase is being deprecated in favour of the MediaWiki Core REST API ([MediaWiki](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikimedia_REST_API)), so the endpoint is a medium-term dependency. A name-only search risks matching the wrong place, so the notes recommend resolving the name and coordinates first, then running a geosearch within roughly 200 to 500 m and requiring name similarity ([API:Geosearch](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/API:Geosearch)). Commons files carry per-file licences (CC BY, CC BY-SA, public domain), so the card must show author and licence. The notes estimate 85 to 95 percent hit rates for famous monuments and under 10 percent for ordinary restaurants and shops. These are estimates, not measurements, and no hit rate was measured for any source in any Indian city.

Below that, the options degrade. Mapillary offers street-level images near the point with a free token, using a bounding box under 0.01 degrees square. Content is CC BY-SA, so credit the username and licence, and the nearest frame may not show the building ([Mapillary API](https://www.mapillary.com/developer/api-documentation), [terms](https://mapillary.com/legal)). Foursquare photos are on paid endpoints, and sources disagree on its free allowance (10,000 Pro calls versus 500) and price ($6.50 versus $18.75 per 1,000). Yelp has no free tier and little India coverage. Both are unsuitable for BYOK free use. Esri World Imagery tiles need no key but the terms conflict: an OSM wiki page says CC BY 4.0 with attribution, while Esri community threads say older free-use conditions changed, with no official answer ([OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Esri), [Esri community](https://community.esri.com/t5/arcgis-location-platform-developers-ques/inquiry-about-world-imagery-service-integration/m-p/1567734)). Treat satellite as a best-effort fallback and label it as a map image, not a photo of the place. A related conflict: the Google Maps Embed API is documented as free but still needing a key and billing account ([Embed overview](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/embed)), yet NEXUS already ships a keyless embed panel (per the repository notes). That keyless `output=embed` pattern is undocumented, so it is a legal and stability risk that should be reviewed before the place card leans on it.

## Fares: show an honest estimate and hand off to the real apps

The fare field needs the most care, because the obvious paths are the riskiest. For Uber, the `/v1.2/estimates/price` endpoint requires Uber-approved access, and Uber states that using it to offer price comparisons with competing services violates §II B of its API Terms ([Uber docs](https://developer.uber.com/docs/riders/references/api/v1.2/estimates-price-get)). Showing Uber beside Ola and Rapido is the comparison it prohibits. Showing Uber alone would still need approval, and whether Uber India accepts new partners in 2026 is unknown. An official Uber MCP endpoint at `mcp.uber.com/claude/rides-3p/mcp` is reported by directory listings, and one summary says US trips only. The notes could not verify it (the directory page returned 403), so treat it as unconfirmed ([Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/integrations/uber)). The official deep link (`setPickup` with pickup, dropoff and `product_id`) works, but a dropoff only displays if a nickname or formatted address is also supplied, and deep links give the user the price inside Uber's app, not inside NEXUS ([Uber deep links](https://developer.uber.com/docs/deep-linking)). For Ola, the notes found Ola Maps (5 million free calls per month for developers in India, per 2024 announcements, status in 2026 unchecked) but that is a maps API, not a fare API ([Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/indias-ola-maps-takes-on-google-maps-with-aggressive-pricing-and-roadmap/)). For Rapido, the only fare tooling is a reverse-engineered `POST /pricing/getFareEstimate` on its PWA, which a later version says now returns encoded binary after a protobuf change. No Rapido deep-link format or business API was checked. Ola's and Rapido's deep-link formats and current developer terms are unverified, so only Uber's button can be built on documented behaviour.

Scraping, reverse-engineered endpoints and phone UI automation are the other routes. Commercial vendors such as Actowiz sell scraped fares (marketing claims, unverified, silent on legality) ([Actowiz](https://www.actowizsolutions.com/uber-ola-rapido-fare-comparison-data-scraping.php)). An academic prototype (arXiv 2512.04065) mirrored Ola's pricing model, used randomly generated Uber test data because API access was restricted, and used Bangalore empirical data for Rapido, tested only in an emulator ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04065v1), [summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/fare-comparison-app-of-uber-ola-and-rapido)). That is evidence that even researchers could not get real prices. The notes found no authoritative source on the legality of scraping these apps and recommend assuming ToS violation and account-ban risk. That is a risk inference, not a legal finding, and none of this is legal advice. The Beckn/ONDC route is open: Namma Yatri is open source and ONDC-connected, but it began in Bengaluru, Hyderabad coverage is unestablished, and querying likely needs registration as a buyer app (the notes' inference) ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2023/03/ondc-onboards-auto-booking-app-namma-yatri)). It is a later project, not a day-one feed.

What can be computed is a range. Telangana revised auto fares in 2026 to ₹30 for the first 1.6 km, ₹14 per extra km, 1.5x from 11 pm to 5 am, and 75 paise per minute waiting ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/state-revises-autorickshaw-fares-1992627)). A conflicting report gives ₹30 for the first 1.9 km and ₹15 per km ([Hyderabad Mail](https://hyderabadmail.com/hyderabad-auto-drivers-fare-hike-bike-taxi-ban-tgpwu/)). The notes could not settle the slab or effective date, so keep the numbers in an editable config and flag them. Auto is not one of the three requested apps, so it is only a sanity anchor. For apps, no official Telangana cab or bike-taxi tariff exists. RGIA prepaid cabs are reported at ₹21 per km by day, ₹25 at night, with a ₹143 minimum for up to 5 km ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/hyderabad-revision-of-fares-of-prepaid-cabs-operating-at-rgia)). Central guidelines reportedly allow surge up to 2x base ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/cab-aggregators-can-charge-up-to-2-times-the-base-fare-during-peak-hours)). A union-claimed 2016 order of about ₹17 per km is unverified. Bike taxis are contested in Telangana, with unions calling white-plate bike taxis illegal and no confirmed tariff ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/rapido-ola-uber-face-backlash-over-bike-taxis-in-hyderabad-3238894), [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/06/25/telangana-riding-bike-taxi-illegal-gig-workers-union-raises-concerns.html)), so the card must not imply that Rapido bike rides are officially sanctioned. Per-app rate cards for Uber, Ola and Rapido in Hyderabad were not found, so any per-app range is an unsourced assumption that the user can edit. The design should therefore show one "typical app cab" range and one "typical bike taxi" range under a banner reading "Estimate, not a live price", with three "Open in Uber / Ola / Rapido" buttons and "Fare unavailable" when distance is missing. It should not print three per-app numbers that imply per-app knowledge.

## Layout at 315 px, adapting to any laptop height

Apple's place-card order is a header image with name, category and rating, then hours, then website, phone and address, then an "Open in Maps" link ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/maps.md)). The notes suggest, as inference rather than sourced fact, promoting the travel chips because they are the product's differentiator. A fixed-width, flexible-height side sheet fits Material guidance, whose 256 to 400 dp width band comes only from secondary sources and was not checked against m3.material.io ([Material side sheets](https://material.io/components/sheets-side)). From top to bottom: a 16:9 photo (about 175 px high, collapsible) with attribution caption; name and category; distance in km with an "approx." tag when haversine; two travel chips (car and bike time); the address; the fare-estimate block with its banner and three app buttons; today's hours and phone if available; then a pinned action row (Directions, Open in Maps, Copy). Provider attribution (Google, OSM/ODbL, Commons author and licence, Mapillary, Esri) sits in a footer line.

Sizing is done in physical pixels from the monitor work area. Tauri exposes `size`, `position`, `work_area` and `scale_factor` per monitor ([tauri-runtime](https://docs.rs/tauri-runtime/latest/src/tauri_runtime/monitor.rs.html)), and tao is per-monitor DPI aware with scale equal to DPI divided by 96 ([tao](https://docs.rs/crate/tao/latest/source/src/platform_impl/windows/dpi.rs)). The notes propose width equal to 315 times the scale factor, so CSS stays 315 px, and height equal to 0.92 of the work-area height clamped between 480 and 1,100 logical pixels. The existing NEXUS sidebar uses monitor height minus 40 px, clamped to 400 to 1,040 logical, with a 12 px right offset (from the repository notes), which is the in-repo precedent. At 1366 by 768 and 125 percent, the usable height is about 560 logical pixels, so the card must stay usable near 480 px: below roughly 560 px, drop the photo and hours to collapsed rows, scroll the body, and pin the action row. Recompute on monitor or scale-factor change events. Mixed-DPI multi-monitor behaviour was not researched. For accessibility, the notes cite WCAG 2.2 AA targets of at least 24 by 24 CSS px for pointer targets and 3:1 contrast for controls and focus indicators ([SC 1.4.11 summary](https://getstark.co/wcag-explained/perceivable/distinguishable/non-text-contrast), [WCAG 2.2 checklist](https://getwcag.com/blog/wcag-2-2-checklist)). Theme tokens, skeleton loading, offline messaging, `aria-live` updates and Escape-to-close are sensible recommendations, but the notes found no sources for them, so treat them as design judgment.

## Confidence and conflicts at a glance

| Field | Primary source | Fallbacks | Confidence it works as described |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distance (km) | Google Routes DRIVE (BYOK) | ORS key, OSRM public, haversine x 1.3 | High for Google and OSRM; haversine factor unsourced |
| Car time | Google Routes (traffic optional) | ORS, OSRM (no traffic) | High, label non-Google as no live traffic |
| Bike time | Google TWO_WHEELER (beta, warning required) | Car time scaled by an assumed band, "about" | Medium for Google in India; low for keyless |
| Address | Google `formattedAddress` | Nominatim (cached, 1/s), Photon | High |
| Photo | Google Photos (on demand, attribution) | Wikipedia/Commons, Mapillary, satellite tiles | High for Google and landmarks; low for shops keyless |
| Fares | None legitimate live | Editable estimate range plus deep links | High that no live feed exists; estimate is low-precision |
| Hours, phone, rating | Google Enterprise fields | OSM extratags | Medium; sparse keyless |

The claims that conflict across the notes are these. The Telangana auto slab is ₹30 for 1.6 km at ₹14 per km in one source and ₹30 for 1.9 km at ₹15 per km in another, unresolved. Foursquare's free allowance and photo price differ across third parties. HERE's free allowance is reported as 30k or 250k transactions per month. Google's grounding MCP is shown with three tools in Google's reference and five in a directory. The Material width band is secondary. Esri's keyless imagery terms conflict. Google's Embed API is documented as key-required while NEXUS ships a keyless embed. The Uber MCP is "US only" in one summary and unverified otherwise. For Google's official remote MCP (`mapstools.googleapis.com/mcp`, with 10,000 free Essentials requests per month, attribution via `placeUrl`, and a visible-sources rule for voice interfaces), the notes recommend calling Routes and Places directly instead, since NEXUS already has the keys and an MCP client ([Google MCP](https://developers.google.com/maps/ai/grounding-lite)). No India coverage statement was found for it. Several figures are third-party only: the March 2025 per-SKU free-cap change and Mapbox's roughly 100,000 free Directions requests per month.

## What must wait, and what carries legal risk

Several items should wait for verification or partnership. Live Uber prices need approved access and are barred from any competitor comparison. The Ola estimate API is invite-only and unconfirmed on Ola's own site. Rapido has no sanctioned feed. ONDC and Namma Yatri quotes need buyer-app onboarding and a Hyderabad coverage check. Ola and Rapido deep-link formats must be read from their own documentation before buttons ship. The Google two-wheeler supported-region list, the TWO_WHEELER and photo SKU tiers, the current Google caching wording (Section 3.2.3(b)) and the India price list must be read before relying on cost claims. A short measurement spike on 30 to 50 real Hyderabad places (monuments, malls, restaurants, shops) should record photo and hours hit rates per source, because every hit-rate figure above is an estimate. The designs to avoid are scraping, reverse-engineered endpoints and phone UI automation, which carry a high ToS and account-ban risk, plus caching Google photo bytes or names and presenting a satellite tile as a photo of the place.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which source gives each fact" to "which facts can NEXUS honestly claim". Four facts can be claimed with provenance labels. The fifth, per-app fares, cannot be claimed at all, and a card that prints three confident prices would be fabricating precision the market does not expose. The design that survives is an estimate range plus deep links, and it should be built so a legitimate feed (Uber's approved API, an Ola partnership, or an ONDC buyer app) could replace the estimate later without changing the layout.

A second implication is that the keyless and BYOK tiers should be treated as two product modes rather than one chain with fallbacks. With a Google key, the card is accurate and cheap (thousands of free cards per month on the 1,000 to 10,000 caps). Without one, shop photos disappear, bike time becomes approximate, and every line carries a quality label. Showing which mode is active (a small "Google" or "OpenStreetMap" source line) is both the attribution those providers require and the honest signal of how much to trust each number.
